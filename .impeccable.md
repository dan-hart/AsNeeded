## Design Context

### Users

People recording as-needed medication doses, reviewing their history, and managing reminders and refills. The core task is to record the right medication and amount with very little effort. Design for people who may need larger text, assistive technology, or extra time to read a screen.

### Brand Personality

Clear, calm, and helpful. The maintainer wants a joyful, easy-to-use native iOS app, using Apple's apps and Human Interface Guidelines as references. Keep warmth in ordinary guidance; use direct, respectful wording for medication limits, errors, permissions, and data replacement.

### Aesthetic Direction

Native SwiftUI on iOS and watchOS, with existing adaptive colors, accent treatment, SF Symbols, and user-selected accessible fonts. Support light and dark appearance, larger text, Reduce Motion, and clear information hierarchy. The yellow App Store screenshot background is marketing artwork, not a replacement for the app's interactive accent.

### Design Principles

1. Put medication names, quantities, units, and the main action before decoration.
2. Explain optional dose limits as the person's saved instructions and recorded history; never imply medical clearance.
3. Prefer familiar native controls with named, comfortably sized actions and alternatives to gestures.
4. Use concise, consistent language and useful empty, permission, loading, and error states.
5. Let essential information wrap and remain reachable at accessibility text sizes; never trade clarity for a fixed card height.
6. Confirm what actually happened. Cancelling, queueing, saving, and sending are different outcomes.

Refine existing flows only to correct demonstrated confusion, friction, accessibility or regression issues; no new visual direction, navigation redesign or decorative effects for this release.
